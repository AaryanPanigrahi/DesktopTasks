Who this covers

DesktopTasks (“the app”) is a desktop widget for Windows that displays your Google Tasks on your desktop. It is published by [YOUR NAME OR ENTITY] (“we”, “us”).

The short version

DesktopTasks runs entirely on your own computer. It reads your Google Tasks and draws them on your desktop. We operate no servers and receive no data from you at any point. Your tasks and your Google credentials are stored only on your own device, and nothing is transmitted anywhere except directly between your computer and Google's own APIs.

What data the app accesses

The app requests a single Google OAuth scope:

ScopeAccess
https://www.googleapis.com/auth/tasks.readonly Read-only access to your Google Tasks

This is the narrowest scope that makes the app function. The app can read your tasks; it cannot create, edit, complete or delete them, and it cannot access any other Google service — not your email, not your files, not your calendar, not your contacts.

Through that scope the app reads:

  • Your task lists (their names and identifiers)
  • The open tasks in those lists, including each task's title, notes, due date, completion status, parent task and sort position

The app also receives and stores an OAuth refresh token from Google, which is what lets it read your tasks again later without asking you to sign in every time.

How the app uses that data

Solely to display your tasks in the widget on your own screen. There is no other use.

Specifically, the app does not:

  • Transmit your Google user data to us or to any third party
  • Send your data to any server we operate — we operate none
  • Use your data for advertising, profiling, or any form of marketing
  • Sell, rent, or trade your data
  • Use your data to train, develop, or improve any machine-learning or artificial-intelligence model
  • Allow any human to read your data, including us
  • Collect analytics, telemetry, crash reports, or usage statistics of any kind

Where that data is stored

Everything is stored locally on your own computer, in files only you and other programs on your machine can read:

WhatWhere
Your tasks, as last fetchedtasks.json in the app's data folder
Your OAuth refresh token and your settingsconfig.json in the same folder
A diagnostic log, when the app has no consoledesktoptasks.log in the same folder

The data folder is %LOCALAPPDATA%\DesktopTasks\ for an installed copy, or the config\ folder inside the project directory when the app is run from source.

The app serves your tasks to its own display window over a web server bound to 127.0.0.1 (your own machine's loopback address). This server is not reachable from your local network or from the internet, it rejects any request that does not originate from your own machine, and it never serves the file containing your refresh token.

How that data is shared

It is not. There is no onward disclosure of your Google user data to any party, for any purpose.

The only network connections the app makes are directly from your computer to Google's own services:

  • accounts.google.com — when you sign in
  • oauth2.googleapis.com — to refresh its access token
  • tasks.googleapis.com — to read your tasks

How long that data is kept, and how to delete it

Your data stays on your computer until you remove it. Nothing expires on our side, because nothing is on our side.

To revoke the app's access to your Google account, visit myaccount.google.com/permissions and remove DesktopTasks. This immediately invalidates its refresh token; the app will no longer be able to read your tasks.

To delete the data stored on your computer, delete the app's data folder (%LOCALAPPDATA%\DesktopTasks\, or the project's config\ folder). That removes your stored tasks, your refresh token, your settings and the log.

Doing both leaves no trace of your Google data anywhere.

No humans read the data except with your affirmative agreement, for security purposes, or to comply with applicable law.

Children

The app is not directed to children and does not knowingly collect data from them. It has no accounts, no profiles and no data collection of its own.

Security

Your Google credentials are never seen by the app: you sign in on Google's own pages, and the app receives only a token afterwards. The sign-in flow uses PKCE and a one-time state parameter, and the token exchange happens over HTTPS directly with Google.

Your refresh token is stored in plain text in a file in your user profile, protected by your operating system's file permissions. Anyone with access to your Windows user account, or to an unencrypted backup of it, could read that file. If that is a concern for you, enable disk encryption and do not share your user account.
