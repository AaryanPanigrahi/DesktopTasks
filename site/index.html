DesktopTasks

Your Google Tasks, pinned to the Windows desktop.

DesktopTasks is a small widget for Windows that shows your Google Tasks on your desktop — behind your icons, out of the way, always there. It survives Win+D, never appears in the taskbar, and resizes itself to fit however many tasks you have.

View on GitHub Read the docs

It runs entirely on your own computer. There is no server, no account to create, and no telemetry. Your tasks are read from Google straight to your machine and stay there. We never receive any of your data, because there is nowhere for it to go.

What it does

  • Shows every task list, with subtasks nested under their parents.
  • Calls out overdue and today, and reads dates the way you would — Today, Tomorrow, then Oct 20.
  • Shows task notes, and says All clear when a list is empty.
  • Pinned behind your desktop icons, with no taskbar or Alt+Tab entry.
  • Sizes itself to its content — no scrolling, no clipped last task.
  • A tray icon for Reload, Reconnect Google, Restart and Quit.

What it can see

DesktopTasks asks for exactly one permission — read-only access to your Google Tasks (tasks.readonly). It can read your task lists and the tasks in them. It cannot change, complete or delete anything, and it cannot touch any other part of your Google account: not your email, not your files, not your calendar, not your contacts.

You can withdraw that access at any time from your Google account permissions. Full detail is in the privacy policy.

Getting it

There is no packaged download yet. For now it runs from source, which needs Python and a Google OAuth client of your own — the README walks through both. 

git clone https://github.com/AaryanPanigrahi/DesktopTasks.git
cd DesktopTasks
cp config/config.example.json config/config.json
python source/google_tasks_setup.py
python source/desktoptasks.py
Signing in shows an “unverified app” warning. That is expected: this app has not been through Google's verification review. Click Advanced → Go to DesktopTasks (unsafe). You are consenting on Google's own page, and nothing here ever sees your password.

Open source

All of it, at github.com/AaryanPanigrahi/DesktopTasks. The fetcher and the sign-in flow are plain Python standard library, so you can read exactly what it sends and where. Issues and pull requests are welcome.
